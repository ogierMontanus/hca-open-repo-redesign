### Reference keys that could not be read: **224**

| Kind | Count | Example | What it is |
|---|---:|---|---|
| non-numeric page | 152 | `VIII` | the page part is not a number |
| bad volume | 71 | `O-226-27` | OCR damage in the volume — `O` for a numeral, `l` for `1` |
| implausible range | 1 | `IV-431-23` | runs backwards, or spans more than 50 pages |

Across **37** register rows, listed in `data/normalized_v094/reference_problems.csv`.

> **Page ranges are expanded, not right-split.** 763 tokens have the form `IX-64-65` or `II-407-09` — a page range whose closing page is abbreviated to its trailing digits, as printed indexes conventionally do. Splitting on the last hyphen reads `II-407-09` as volume `II-407`, page `09`: silent corruption rather than visible failure. They are expanded to one pair per page, which is why the reference counts above exceed the raw token counts.

> `seq` in the emitted `references.csv` is a positional counter over each row's key list. It is **not** the live `seq`, which records order of mention. V0.94 cannot express that, so it cannot be recovered from it.

## What this says

- The **works register has arrived** and its identifiers carry, so works can be adopted on evidence rather than on faith.
- The **place register cannot be adopted on its ids**; its attributes (country, category) are the valuable part and can be joined on label.
- **Persons are absent**, so V0.82 remains the person spine regardless of anything else here.
- **Empty columns are not future-proofing**: coordinates and cross-references are provisioned and unpopulated, so every derivation that fills them today must stay.
- **Reference ordering is lost** in V0.94's encoding and cannot be reconstructed — only re-invented.
